Life in Words, May 2026, Part Three

Today, you can not only talk to a computer, but also give it a task and it will carry it out. This helps programmers, and it can also be applied to other digitized materials, such as text documents. The output depends mainly on how well the task is written. Sometimes you can learn from it, other times it needs to be guided. It’s fast, but people won’t eat any faster; too many tasks can easily fill up a person’s capacity for thinking and decision-making before the day is even over.

I created an assistant and gave it a full name and date of birth. This information was necessary so that it could have its own email address. It will have other assistants under it, each specialized in their own tasks. Simply put, imagine that every app on your phone turns into something you can talk to instead of tapping on the screen. For example, one assistant for contacts, another for the calendar, and so on.

I communicate with the main AI, and it communicates with the others depending on what information I need or what task it’s working on. It essentially manages this on its own, just as I’ve programmed it to do. Programming is telling the computer what to do. There’s no need to be afraid of it—they aren’t human. Artificial intelligence is just statistics and simulates human speech. It only knows what has been written by humans so far; it doesn’t think for itself. You can’t make something alive out of something inanimate.

I had my assistant’s right-hand man study my favorite books. Then it occurred to me to ask what advice he would give me regarding the loss of my long-term job and what to do next—whether, based on those books, it would align with how I had already decided in my own mind. I was surprised because the answer matched my own decisions.

I’d like to point out two things. Artificial intelligence reflects how a person feeds it information and essentially writes what the person wants to hear. It’s a good idea to configure it to be more critical when the task or question is something that might be nonsense. The second important thing is not to leave decision-making entirely up to it and not to become merely an executor. A person is shaped, among other things, by the decisions they make, and if they stop making them, they can easily lose their way.

To summarize the assistant’s response in my own words: when you receive something, you can lose it, and that loss doesn’t define who you are. Keep focusing on your work and your plans. Don’t jump right into looking for a job; figure out your next steps. Put yourself out there and stay true to what you don’t yet understand. To quote the conclusion: “What you’ve built your life on was never meant to bear such a burden; now is the time to find out what can.”

It confirmed to me that the way I chose to handle this life situation is in line with what is written in the books I believe in. Even before I asked this question, those decisions came to me—simply put—instinctively and gradually. It was as if someone had read my life story and pointed me in the direction to move forward.

When the blow came, I wasn’t afraid of what would happen next. I didn’t lose myself; I only lost my job. I’m the one who does the work there, not the other way around. I carried on as if nothing had happened. I still go there. I’m open to sharing my projects and knowledge. If my time there is coming to an end, then let it end as best as it can.

Meanwhile, I’m getting ready to take the first step. I don’t want to get into debt. I prefer a more cautious approach rather than taking risks. I laid out a piece of paper in front of me and placed job hunting at the very bottom of the grid. If I started looking right away, I would be wasting my own and others’ time, because only then would what I really need begin to take shape. I added more squares and connected them so that they all lead to the bottom one. It's basically what I've been doing, what I need for the interview, how I envision the job, how I'll go about looking for it, and who I'll share my plan with.

Fear is a part of every human being and manifests itself in various forms. There are methods for keeping it under control or suppressing it without emotion. But emotions are also part of us. When a person separates themselves from these and other traits in order to be objective, they are left in complete emptiness and utterly alone. There is no need to fear what is happening or other people. It is enough to fear what created life, because if it can create it, it can also take it away. If I have to choose between evolution and something more intelligent above it, I’ll go with the option where the fear and emotions it evokes are more pleasant.
