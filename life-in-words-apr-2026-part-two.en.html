An employee will always be limited by the amount of their salary. It doesn’t matter whether that salary is low or high. Any extra effort put in isn’t taken into account and doesn’t reflect the person’s actual financial compensation. With your own business, income can fluctuate, and there is no guarantee of monthly stability in the form of a paycheck. I’m interested in whether, on average, a person can earn more through a business, and I’m even more interested in whether they can gain greater freedom with their time.

What should a forecast for the coming years look like? Do I want to play the fortune-teller here, or the scientist who calculates thousands of years into the past and future? No, I can’t even say what will happen tomorrow. It’s not that I don’t plan—the calendar is my source of truth—but experience tells me straight up: a plan is something that can easily change.

However, I’d like to be clear about what would make the work I invest my time in more enjoyable. And let’s face it: we don’t live for thousands of years to try everything. The older I get, the more it seems to me that time passes faster between day and night.

It’s as if an airplane pilot welcomed his passengers by telling them the flight would take an hour. After half an hour, he’d announce that he’s picking up speed and we’ll get there sooner. After fifteen minutes, he’d say he’s picked up speed again and we’ll get there even sooner. And this is how life flies toward its ultimate purpose—death.

We are, after all, among the living, and even though not everyone has exactly the same conditions to start something bigger than themselves, they should definitely try to do it their own way at least once. I recall the words of someone from a few years ago: "Go to Canada, because you won’t be satisfied until you’re there," as if I heard it yesterday. Besides those words, other people also encouraged me to go, and so I gathered my courage. It was a several-year adventure that took me all the way to the Philippines and back home.

I proved it back then, so I’d just be fooling myself if I thought I couldn’t do it now with my own company. I asked people who had started their own businesses. Not a single one of them would go back to being an employee. Of course, there are certainly people who went back to being employees because things didn’t work out for them. But you can spare a year of your life for an experiment like this.
